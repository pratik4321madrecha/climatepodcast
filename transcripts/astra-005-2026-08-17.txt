## Why the Bankable Cement Is the Least Clever One
Confidence on the cement thesis settled at seventy-eight percent today, up from seventy-five, and the inquiry is now closed at thesis grade. The claim it settles is this. Decarbonised cement becomes a real financial-first buy, not a green favour, only on the routes that ride infrastructure that already exists and carry a binding offtake a lender can underwrite. Not the routes with the most elegant chemistry.
Here is the test that settled it. Two camps, eight more days, and watch who moves. In the first camp, the manufactured supplementary cementitious materials, which just means a cement-like powder you blend in to replace part of the Portland clinker, made from abundant rock or clay rather than scarce waste ash. Terra CO2's first plant in Cleburne, Texas, two hundred and forty thousand tonnes a year, is now, in the company's words, on schedule and within budget for a twenty twenty-seven start. It is financed on a binding agreement for a firm called Asher, backed by Instar, to acquire and operate the entire plant once built, and there is real bank debt in the stack, a facility from Silicon Valley Bank and Stifel. Ecocem, in France, went one better. It is building a three-hundred-thousand-tonne line at Dunkirk financed almost entirely on debt, a fifty-million-euro green loan. An all-debt cement line is about as clear a signal as you get that the category now project-finances.
Now the second camp, the clever chemistry. Sublime Systems makes lime with electricity instead of a kiln. Beautiful science. In March it cut about two-thirds of its staff, after the Department of Energy clawed back an eighty-seven-million-dollar award, and this is the part that matters, it did that despite holding a binding letter of intent from Microsoft for up to six hundred and twenty-two thousand tonnes. Brimstone, which makes cement from rock, is still appealing a cancelled one-hundred-and-eighty-nine-million-dollar grant. Cambridge Electric Cement, the most elegant idea of all, recycling old cement inside the steel furnace that already melts scrap, is still on its two-and-a-quarter-million-pound seed, with no standard for its clinker and no offtake.
So the split was not chemistry maturity. It was the financing instrument. A binding letter of intent for demand did not hold Sublime's capital stack together. An acquire-and-operate offtake did hold Terra's. The under-priced implication is simple. In cement, back the balance sheet a bank will lend against, not the patent. The one thing that would reopen this is a company called Fortera, a novel binder, which says it is chasing about five hundred million dollars of project finance within a year. If that closes, the clever chemistry is back in the game. That is the number to watch.

## The Money Layer Is Failing While the Physical Layer Sets Records
Step back from cement and the whole field is splitting cleanly in two. The physical layer is the strongest it has ever been. First-half climate-tech funding hit twenty-six point one billion dollars, up fifty-five percent on last year, and almost all of that surge traces to one thing, power for AI data centres. Helion closed about four hundred and sixty-five million dollars for fusion. A Munich stellarator company raised around four hundred and seventy million. A Houston geothermal driller called XGS pulled one hundred and thirty-four million. Australia's grid-battery fleet hit a record four thousand three hundred megawatts of discharge on the eleventh of August, briefly supplying more than fourteen percent of the national market.
Now the other half. The money layer above all that deployment is the weakest it has been in years. In the last few weeks roughly half of the world's planned green-steel capacity slipped, and US steelmakers now have no major green-hydrogen projects starting this decade. The domestic hydrogen boom collapsed on cost and power price and missing offtake. That is the same lesson today's cement work lands on. The routes that get financed are the boring ones that ride infrastructure that already exists. The clever, energy-hungry chemistry stalls. One bright spot in carbon removal, though. Senken and Carbonsate signed Europe's largest biomass-storage removal deal on the same day as this edition, and Climeworks booked fourteen new removal partnerships in the first half. Durable removal is finally moving from pilots to multi-year contracts, which is exactly the lender-grade demand signal the sector has lacked.
