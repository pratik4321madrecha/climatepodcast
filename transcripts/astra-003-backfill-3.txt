The spine · thesis (D2 interrogation)
Start with the new material, because it changes the map: a radiative-evaporative hybrid hydrogel dissipates heat even in humid air where a passive film cannot. A dry radiative cooler only works while the sky window is open, the 8 to 13 micrometre band where the atmosphere is transparent, and water vapour closes that window, so in damp air the film loses its heat sink. The hybrid gel sidesteps the closed window entirely by adding a second exit for heat: it holds water in a polymer network laced with a hygroscopic salt (lithium chloride), radiates in the infrared AND lets that water evaporate to carry away latent heat, then pulls moisture back out of the night air to recharge. It is closer to a smart swamp cooler with a photonic skin than to a paint. The REC hydrogel (Meijie Chen, Central South University and Brown) runs 12.0°C below a radiative-only film and 20.9°C below bare substrate outdoors, above ambient and in humid air - https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40820-025-01903-0, with a companion latent-radiative thermostat gel - https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42548-0 and a dynamic evaporative-radiative switch - https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aed6069 doing the same.
We went in asking whether radiative cooling pays back broadly as a passive film; we now believe that strong form is wrong at 38% confidence, down from 50%, because the interrogation hardened every wall rather than softening it. The humidity fix arrives as a water-cycling consumable, not the hypothesised film, so it opens the humid market in physics while resetting the product and the moat. The best financial-first duty cycle turns out to be narrow and specific. And the commodity coating is being won in Asia while the Western integrator has no priced venture round. The technology is more capable than a year ago; the investment thesis as originally stated is less true.

The one duty cycle that clears the financial-first bar is the AI data centre in a hot-dry, water-scarce site, and it clears it for reasons a hyperscaler already pays for. Cooling is about 40% of data-centre energy, and a radiative-plus-AC hybrid delivers roughly 28% average annual cooling-energy savings at 40 to 50 W/m² peak - https://www.energy-proceedings.org/radiative-cooling-technology-for-data-center-cooling-systems/. The second bill matters more: in arid sites an evaporative cooling tower can burn tens of thousands of litres of water a day, so a dry radiative assist cuts electricity AND water together, in exactly the hot-dry air where the sky window stays open. It bolts onto an existing dry-cooler capex line rather than needing a greenfield case - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306261924000552. So what: pick the duty cycle where hot, high-utilisation, above-ambient, dry and water-stressed all line up, and radiative cooling stops being a climate nicety and becomes a cost line, the inverse of the monsoon-roof problem.

The bankability gate is still uncrossed, and the primary documents are worse than session one assumed. SkyCool, the best Western integrator, has no closed Series A - https://www.propelx.com/blog/skycool-systems-radiative-cooling-climate-tech/: as of May 2026 it is targeting a $10 to 15M round, with only about $5 to 11M raised, mostly grants and seed. Its data-centre push, the venture-scale bet, is grant-staged, a proposed $1.8M California Energy Commission demo plus a BNEF Pioneers finalist slot and a national-lab pilot, with gross margin and payback still undisclosed - https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/166613-23. So what: the wedge is real but the money in it is still catalytic grant capital, and a first audited hyperscale payback disclosed at those 2026 to 2027 demos is the single event that would flip this from niche to scaling.
The commodity coating is a scale game Asia is already winning, so the film is the wrong layer to own. i2Cool, an HKUST spinout, closed a $12.8M Series A across 13 investors - https://www.hkstp.org/en/park-life/news-and-events/news/i2cool-attracts-close-to-hkd100-million-in-series-a, its iPaint hits 95% reflectivity, and a Hong Kong mall deployment cut rooftop temperature 30.3°C and monthly AC energy 42%, beside commodity sprayables and paint majors (Asian Paints, Berger) selling high-reflectance paint at hardware-store prices. The Western venture champion has no priced round while the Asian coating player has $12.8M and paying malls. This is the perovskite and sodium-cell pattern again: Asia commoditises the commodity layer, and the durable rent moves to durability, integration and colour.
The one genuinely UK-ownable seat is Cambridge science with no company on it: coloured, biodegradable radiative cooling. Every white-film seat is taken or commoditised, but the adoption ceiling on radiative cooling has always been that customers do not want white roofs and white facades. Silvia Vignolini's Cambridge group makes structural colour from self-assembled cellulose nanocrystals - https://vignolini.group.ch.cam.ac.uk/cellulose-nanocrystal-self-assembly, a plant-derived, biodegradable material that produces vivid colour from geometry rather than pigment and still cools by day. It is world-leading science with no startup on it, the textbook "UK invents, Asia deploys" gap. The contestable seats that remain, then, are three and none of them is the film: heat-rejection systems integration for data centres and cold chain, durable humidity-tolerant evaporative-hybrid materials, and coloured biodegradable coatings.

Everyone is still pitching the film; the value already left it. Three things most observers miss. First, the killer app is not a roof at all, it is dry heat-rejection for AI data centres in hot-dry, water-scarce regions, where the technology cuts the two bills a hyperscaler is already paying and dry-sky physics finally points the same way as demand. Second, the humidity unlock is a water-cycling hydrogel consumable, not a paint, which resets the moat from materials IP to water-management and service economics, and its IP sits unowned in Chinese and US academic labs, a licensing window. Third, the coating commoditises to Asia, so a Western entrant must own the non-film layers or lose. Deal-sourcing screen: back data-centre and cold-chain integration teams and the coloured or humidity-tolerant materials plays; pass any generic-photonic-film-for-generic-roof pitch, and treat SkyCool's first priced round and first audited data-centre payback as the two tells that re-rate the whole category.
WIDE LENS · STATE OF CLIMATE TECH
Compute's power hunger is now the demand curve the whole system is pricing against. The US DOE's draft transmission study frames a roughly $1.1 trillion build driven by data-centre load against a 2,600 GW interconnection backlog - https://www.energy.gov/oe/articles/does-office-electricity-publishes-2026-draft-national-transmission-needs-study, and a US heatwave exposed the same crunch on water, with one campus evaporating millions of gallons a day - https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/3/us-heatwave-raises-alarms-over-ai-data-centre-energy-demands. That is precisely why today's data-centre dry-cooling wedge matters: firm capacity and water are the scarce goods, and anything that shaves both at a hot site is bid up.
Europe finally has a fusion champion, and the capital wall is breaking on this side of the Atlantic. Munich's Proxima Fusion raised €411M at a €2.4B valuation, the largest European fusion round on record - https://www.proximafusion.com/press-news/proxima-fusion-raises-eu411-million-to-build-europes-commercial-fusion-champion, with Google and RWE strategic. Fusion has become a fundable engineering-scale-up asset, and the UK's Tokamak Energy and First Light now have a continental benchmark to position against.
Carbon removal is consolidating into a few offtake-backed winners. Frontier doubled its advance-purchase pool to $1.8B, with Anthropic joining - https://www.esgtoday.com/frontier-raises-over-900-million-for-carbon-removal-investments-from-google-anthropic-and-others/, but narrowed to 10 to 15 partners on 8 to 10-year contracts. Huge capital, tiny delivered volumes: the market is bifurcating into a handful of bankable suppliers and a demandless long tail.
UK and European deep-tech climate VC is alive and materials-led, and Cambridge is a live node. Cambridge's CuspAI is closing $400M at a $2.6B valuation, backed by Bezos Expeditions and Kleiner Perkins - https://www.businessweekly.co.uk/posts/amazon-chief-bezos-backing-cuspai-400m-round-his-second-major-cambridge-investment, running AI plus molecular simulation for materials discovery, while London's Gaussion raised $28M for battery energy-intelligence. Dealflow clusters around AI-for-materials, the exact template a coloured-cooling or humidity-tolerant materials startup would follow.
Solar's tech keeps winning while its financing stack cracks. HZB set a 25.5% perovskite-CIGS tandem world record - https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/07/01/german-researchers-achieve-world-record-25-5-efficiency-for-perovskite-cigs-tandem-solar-cell/, yet a top US residential installer, Freedom Forever, filed bankruptcy affecting about 190,000 systems. Value migrates to cells, software and firm-power integration, and away from the fragile install-and-finance layer, the same "own the durable layer, not the commodity" logic running through today's spine.
Long view. Over the next 12 to 36 months the binding constraint shifts from generation cost to deliverable firm capacity, interconnection, water and offtake, with AI data-centre load the marginal buyer setting prices and underwriting supply directly. Capital concentrates into fewer, larger bets, and Europe keeps punching above its weight in AI-for-materials and firm power. Radiative cooling rides that curve only where it attaches to the data-centre and cold-chain heat-rejection line, not the roof.
Ecosystem placement
Two electric demand curves dominate the transition, cooling and compute, and they now intersect inside the data centre. Cooling is already about a fifth of global building electricity and the AC base is set to more than triple by 2050; compute is the fastest-growing single load on the grid. Radiative cooling's real role is load-and-water avoidance at the hot-dry, high-utilisation node where those two curves meet: it sits alongside efficiency, storage and demand response as a way to shave cooling load and water draw before they hit the wires or the aquifer. Its placement is conditional, not universal, because the map of where it works, hot and dry, only partly overlaps the map of where cooling demand explodes, hot and humid. The transition needs both this and a humid-air answer that, today, exists only as a lab-stage consumable.
The consensus sells radiative cooling as a paint for hot roofs. Its best market is AI data centres in hot-dry, water-stressed regions, and its humidity fix is a water-cycling hydrogel, not a better film. So anyone betting on "own the photonic film" is betting on the one layer that already lost: the Western champion (SkyCool) has no priced Series A while the Asian coating player (i2Cool) has $12.8M and paying malls.
