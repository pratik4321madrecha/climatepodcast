## Why the 40% TPV Cell Is the Wrong Number
Our confidence that the durable rent in thermophotovoltaic thermal batteries lodges in the cell moved down again today, from thirty-five percent to twenty-six. The refined belief, that value migrates to the integrated, contracted system, hardened from seventy-two to seventy-seven percent.
Start with what the thing is. A thermophotovoltaic thermal battery stores cheap or curtailed electricity as heat in a solid carbon block at up to two thousand four hundred degrees Celsius, then converts that glow back to electricity using a thermophotovoltaic cell. That cell is a photovoltaic cell tuned to infrared, a two-junction indium-gallium tandem with a gold mirror behind it that recycles about ninety-three percent of the photons it cannot use. Think of a solar panel aimed at a furnace instead of the sun. It hits forty-one percent in the lab, forty-four in the best air-bridge design.
Here is the problem. Forty-four percent is the cell efficiency, not the product. Electricity in to electricity out, the round-trip, is only twenty to forty-five percent, because the integrated cell efficiency itself falls to around twenty to thirty-five percent once it is in a real system. Lithium is eighty-five to ninety. You throw away most of every electron. As a merchant electricity store, it fails on the economics.
Now the evidence that settled where the money is. Antora's flagship patent claims the system, the movable heat engines that slide across a stationary carbon block to decouple power from energy capacity. It does not claim the cell. A company that believed the rent lived in the cell would have patented the cell. They patented the box. And their one disclosed revenue contract is a heat-arbitrage tariff with a utility that pays them to charge on surplus renewables. There is no cell in that revenue path.
The implication for an investor is direct. Back the operator who signs the bankable heat offtake, not the company with the best cell. Merchant indium-gallium has a documented path to forty cents a watt at volume, so the cell commoditizes by the late twenties. The cell is a cost lever anyone can buy. It is margin, not a moat.
