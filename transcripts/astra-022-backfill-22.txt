The Workshop · how the cell actually works
Aluminium smelting has burned its own electrode for 140 years, and the inert anode is the first credible way to stop. The Hall-Heroult process (the only industrial route to primary aluminium) dissolves alumina in molten cryolite at about 960°C and runs current through it using a block of carbon as the anode, the positive electrode. That carbon is not a spectator: oxygen stripped from the alumina attacks it, so the anode burns to CO2 at roughly 1.5 tonnes of CO2 per tonne of metal and is fed in continuously. An inert anode swaps the consumable carbon for a metal-ceramic electrode that does not burn; it evolves oxygen gas instead of CO2. Same cell, same alumina, but the exhaust is O2. Picture replacing a candle wick that shortens as it burns with a filament that glows and stays.
On the narrow question of does it work, the answer is now a settled yes. ELYSIS, the Rio Tinto and Alcoa joint venture, ran a commercial-size 450 kA cell at Alma, Quebec in November 2025 - https://elysis.com/en/elysis-achieves-breakthrough-with-commercial-size-cell-a-first-in-aluminium-production-using-the, producing metal at P1020 purity (the standard commodity grade, 99.7% aluminium). RUSAL commissioned its second industrial line at Krasnoyarsk in March 2026 - https://realtynmore.com/rusal-commissions-worlds-first-inert/ under its ALLOW INERTA brand, roughly 6,000 cumulative tonnes since 2017. Two independent industrial lines, on two continents, making salable metal with oxygen coming off the anode. The electrochemistry Alcoa abandoned in the 1980s on durability grounds is no longer the blocker.

We went in asking whether a rational smelter buys inert anodes to make metal cheaper, not greener. We now believe it does not, at 24% confidence, down from 50%. The tell is a silence. Current efficiency (the share of current that actually deposits metal rather than being wasted) and kWh/kg (the direct-current energy to make one kilogram) are the two numbers any operating-cost win would let a producer publish to take share. Neither ELYSIS nor RUSAL has released either, nine months and roughly 6,000 cumulative tonnes in. ELYSIS repeats only a qualitative claim that its anodes last 30 times longer than carbon.

The independent physics runs the wrong way for a cost story. A 2026 peer-reviewed review in the Journal of the Electrochemical Society - https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/ace332 puts inert-anode current efficiency at 60 to 92% against carbon's 90%-plus, the minimum energy at about 9.16 kWh/kg versus 6.25 for the carbon cell, and operating voltage up 1.15 V. Its own conclusion: no definitive OPEX savings. The oxygen the anode evolves is the same oxygen that used to burn the carbon and release useful heat; take that reaction away and you pay for the heat with electricity. That is where the 47% penalty comes from, and it is a chemistry fact, not a scale-up bug that a bigger cell fixes.

When smelters spend their own money in 2026, they buy carbon. EGA, a top-five producer with no captive green mandate, broke ground with Sunstone on a 300,000 t/yr carbon-anode plant in the UAE - https://www.alcircle.com/press-release/ega-and-sunstone-to-begin-construction-of-anode-manufacturing-plant-in-uae-in-2026-116594, first metal around 2028, while Rio's real capital went to the $1.5B carbon-anode AP60 expansion. Inert metal instead sells on a green premium that is thin and barely tradeable: the LME finalised its low-carbon methodology in February 2026 - https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/ten-key-points-on-low-carbon-steel-aluminium-markets/, tightening the threshold from 10 to 8 tonnes CO2e per tonne (the LME premium being the upcharge a mill pays for verified low-carbon metal), but it is an assessment framework, not a liquid contract, and spot often shows little or no upcharge. And P1020 (the P1020A commodity benchmark, 99.7% aluminium) is the ceiling: inert metal lands at 99.4 to 99.7%, so aerospace at 99.9%-plus is structurally excluded.
So the durable model is a green-brand compliance asset, owned by incumbents, sold on carbon intensity, not a cost-down. The only tradeable near-term seat is the enabling-materials layer beneath the smelter, not the smelter and not the metal. That reframes the whole opportunity, and it is the point the rest of this edition builds on.
VERDICT · WEAKENED
The technology works and is industrial. The 2026-30 merchant financial-first thesis does not. Confidence 28% → 24% (run 50 → 38 → 32 → 28 → 24).
Most observers price inert anodes as a part swap that kills smelting's CO2. It is a full cell redesign whose economics are best exactly where the grid is already clean, so it decarbonises the hydro smelters that were already low-carbon and barely touches the coal-powered tonnes that emit most. The deal-sourcing filter that follows: do not underwrite the smelter or the metal, underwrite the instrument. The corrosion-resistant Ni-Fe-Cu alloys, the wettable cathodes, and above all the at-amperage wear and current-efficiency diagnostics that every anode developer needs and none will publish, that is where a defensible, buyer-neutral position sits.

Where it sits in the system
Primary aluminium is roughly 2% of global emissions, and most of that is the power feeding the pot, not the burning anode. That geometry is the whole story: an inert anode removes the process-CO2 slice but leaves the electricity slice untouched, so it delivers most where the grid is already clean and least where coal-fired smelters emit hardest. In a transition that is increasingly about firming clean electrons and qualifying materials, inert-anode aluminium is a downstream compliance product waiting on the grid, while the live value is in the electrochemistry-diagnostics toolchain that any metals decarbonisation has to run through.
Contrarian Take
Everyone treats inert anodes as swapping a part to kill smelting's CO2. The primary sources say it is a full cell redesign whose economics are best exactly where the grid is already clean, so it cleans up the smelters that were already clean unless the grid is fixed first. And the tell that settles it is a silence: two industrial producers, years of commercial-grade metal, and not one has published the current efficiency. A firm with an operating-cost win publishes the number to take share. The non-disclosure is the disclosure.
