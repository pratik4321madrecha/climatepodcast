THE SPINE · WORKSHOP
A thermophotovoltaic thermal battery stores electricity as heat and reads it back as light. You run cheap or curtailed electricity through a resistive heater to warm a stack of graphite blocks to well over 2,000°C, hold that heat for hours or days behind insulation, then, when you want power back, you open a shutter and let the glowing block radiate onto a thermophotovoltaic cell - https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy22osti/80222.pdf, a semiconductor that converts that infrared glow straight to electricity with no moving parts. Think of it as a solar panel aimed at a synthetic sun you charged up yourself. The physics that decides the whole game is not the block, which is trivially cheap, but how much of the emitted light the cell turns back into electrons.
The cell genuinely crossed 40%, and it did so by managing photons, not by inventing a new semiconductor. A two-junction III-V tandem tuned to 1.4 and 1.2 eV, sitting over a gold back-reflector that bounces roughly 93% of the useless sub-bandgap photons back into the block to be re-absorbed, hit 41.1% at a 2,400°C emitter - https://www.nrel.gov/grid/news/program/2022/capturing-light-from-heat-at-40-percent-efficiency-nrel-makes-big-strides-in-thermophotovoltaics in the MIT and NREL demonstration. Reflector engineering then reached 44% below 1,500°C with an air-bridge design - https://www.pv-magazine.com/2024/05/27/u-s-scientists-develop-air-bridge-thermophotovoltaic-cells-with-44-efficiency/. This is the part that works. The record moved because someone got better at recycling wasted light, which is why it will keep creeping up.
"A 40%-efficient cell is not a 40% round-trip. You can discard sixty of every hundred electrons and still win, but only if the buyer was never paying you for the electrons."
ASTRA · INQ-027 VERDICT
Round-trip efficiency is where the strong thesis broke. Electricity-in to electricity-out is charge (~99%) times high-temperature storage losses times TPV discharge (~40%), which lands the whole loop near 35%, and the published latent-heat TPV-battery techno-economic analysis puts an optimised round-trip only above 20% - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0196890425013548. That is a system number, not a cell number, and no cell record closes a fifty-point gap against lithium. So we went in asking whether a 40% cell makes thermal batteries a lithium-competitive electricity store; we now believe it does not, at lower confidence, because the arithmetic of the round-trip is fixed by the discharge step and lithium keeps its 85-90%.

The reason the business is still real is that the cost curve lives in a different metric than lithium's. Graphite storage media sits inside the DOE's ultra-long-duration target, so a thermal battery buys energy capacity an order of magnitude cheaper than a lithium wall. When the customer pays for stored hours rather than for cheap cycling, that trade flips from a weakness to a moat. VERDICT · WEAKENED (STRONG FORM), REFINED THESIS HOLDS
The product works and the business is real, but it is a firm-heat and firm-power technology, not a lithium-competitive electricity store. Confidence 50% → 35% on the strong form; the refined heat/firm-power thesis sits near 72%.
Most observers are pricing the cell. The value is in the contract. A 35%-round-trip box that also throws off 1,800°C heat is a better neighbour for a GPU hall than a 90%-round-trip lithium wall that throws off nothing useful, because the data-centre buyer is paying for firmness and co-located heat, where round-trip is not the metric on the term sheet. The deal-sourcing filter this gives you: back the integrated firm on a signed heat offtake or firm-power PPA, and ignore anyone pitching a merchant TPV cell as the moat.
THE WIDE LENS · STATE OF CLIMATE TECH
Firm power for AI is now the gravitational centre of climate capital. H1'26 climate VC hit $26.1bn, up 55% year on year - https://www.ctvc.co/h126-climate-tech-funding-up-55-to-26bn-thanks-to-data-centers/, with nuclear fission and fusion taking 44% of the energy total and $470m going into a Munich stellarator. Every dollar is chasing whatever puts 24/7 electrons into a GPU hall, and Antora rhymes with it.
The grid, not generation cost, is the binding constraint. PJM finally greenlit a large tranche of clean power - https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/clean-energy/pjm-has-finally-greenlit-a-lot-of-new-clean-power-can-it-get-built, but interconnection and equipment queues decide whether any of it gets built. This is exactly why sited, behind-the-meter firm heat looks attractive: it routes around the queue.
Carbon-removal demand is softening even as supply scales. Heirloom committed $475M to two Louisiana DAC plants - https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/carbon-removal-top-startups-fundraising, but Microsoft paused new engineered-CDR credit buying and tilted to nature-based offsets. The biggest buyer wobbling is a real demand-side signal under the whole category.
Materials, not devices, are where the science frontier moved this sweep. A monolithic perovskite/CIGS tandem cleared 30% - https://www.nature.com/nenergy/articles?type=article&year=2026, a tungsten-diselenide catalyst pushed Li-air toward workable oxygen kinetics, and an Ulm and Jena team showed a solar-to-hydrogen material that releases H2 in the dark.
Long view: over 12-36 months the centre of gravity keeps migrating from cheap generation to firm, sited, dispatchable power co-located with compute. The investable asset becomes the contracted revenue floor (heat offtake, firm-power PPA, cap-and-floor), not the cleverest cost curve. That is the through-line joining LDES and thermal batteries.
Where this sits in the whole system: a thermal battery is not competing with lithium for the frequency-response and daily-arbitrage jobs lithium already owns. It is competing with a gas boiler for industrial heat and, increasingly, with a gas turbine or a grid connection for firm data-centre power. That places it on the demand side of the decarbonisation stack, converting cheap intermittent electrons into the one product a factory or a GPU hall cannot buy off the merchant market, which is dispatchable heat and firmness at a fixed price.
Everyone reads "Antora $550M plus 40% TPV" as thermal batteries now beating lithium at storing electricity. They do not, and Antora is not claiming to. The real bet is that the AI build-out makes firm, sited power co-located with recoverable heat the scarce product, so a 35%-round-trip box that also emits 1,800°C heat beats a 90%-round-trip lithium wall that emits nothing useful. The cell is a red herring for value capture. The contracted heat and power floor is the asset.
