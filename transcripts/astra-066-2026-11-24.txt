## UK quantum's largest new money is the state as buyer, while private rounds went to photonics and physics AI
In AI for science and materials, Cambridge Innovation Capital led a ten million dollar seed extension in BeyondMath in February, which took its seed round to eighteen and a half million dollars. BeyondMath builds a physics foundation model, one large AI model trained on the equations of physics, so that an engineer gets a simulation in minutes instead of hours. The company says it runs up to a thousand times faster than supercomputer methods. That is the company's own claim.
In quantum, chips and photonics, Salience Labs of Oxford raised a one hundred and twenty-five million dollar Series B on the fourteenth of October, led by Bullhound Capital with the venture arms of AMD and Intel. Salience makes optical circuit switches, devices that steer light from one fibre to another without turning it into electricity and back. The announcement gives no figures for signal loss or switching speed, so the product cannot yet be judged from public numbers. Universal Quantum of Brighton raised over one hundred million dollars in a Series A on the eighth of October. ## UK quantum confidence moves from sixty-six to sixty-eight percent because the new money is all state buying
Confidence in the hypothesis that UK quantum demand is still mostly bought by the state moved from sixty-six to sixty-eight percent. The verdict stays leaning confirmed.
Around the sixth of November the government and UKRI announced one hundred and thirty-eight million pounds for cross-government quantum sensing procurement, thirty-five million pounds for networking testbeds with calls opening in February, and twelve million pounds for a national skills centre. The thirty million pounds from the Ministry of Defence sits inside the one hundred and thirty-eight. Each line is a purchaser, a testbed or a trainer, not a private customer.
Quantum sensing means using atoms or defects whose energy levels shift with a magnetic field to measure that field very precisely. It is the quantum field closest to being sold. The deciding number for any sensor is sensitivity per unit time against a classical sensor, and none was available this week.
The report on the quantum hubs says about three hundred million pounds of public money from 2014 to 2024 drew about four hundred and eighty-two million pounds of private investment and seventy-four spinouts. That is about one point six pounds private per public pound, my division. It fits two readings: the state leads, or the state is a cheap first customer that makes private bets safer. The sources cannot separate them.
The test that would change the verdict is a UK-owned quantum hardware company with paying customers that are neither a state body nor a state-funded lab.

## Photonics is where private money showed up this autumn, and Cambridge is building a quantum base
Three threads this week. First, UK quantum is state-bought: procurement, testbeds and skills. Second, the private money I could verify went to photonic networking for AI datacentres, not to quantum computing. Third, Cambridge is adding quantum capacity: its university-linked fund hired a founding quantum chief executive as an entrepreneur in residence in July. Over the next one to three years the question is whether sensing and photonic networking find non-state customers before quantum computing has to justify itself through procurement alone.
