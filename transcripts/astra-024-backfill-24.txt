THE SPINE · WORKSHOP
Caloric cooling pumps heat by squeezing or stretching a solid, with no refrigerant gas anywhere in the loop. The caloric effect is simple: a solid's temperature and entropy shift when you apply a field. Barocaloric - https://barocal.com/about-us/ devices put hydrostatic pressure on "plastic crystals" like neopentylglycol; elastocaloric ones strain shape-memory alloys such as nitinol; electrocaloric ones apply an electric field to ferroelectrics. Squeeze the material and it heats; dump that heat to ambient; release the pressure and the material cools below where it started; draw heat from the cold side. It is a heat pump built from a block of solid, not a compressor and a canister of HFC.
The materials are genuinely real and improving fast - this is where the excitement is earned. Neopentylglycol showed a "colossal" barocaloric entropy change of ΔS ~389 J/kg/K near room temperature - https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09730-9 back in 2019. The 2025-26 work pushed harder: organic ionic plastic crystals now reach ΔS of 92-240 J/kg/K with pressure sensitivity up to 23.7 K/kbar - https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adq8396, and a neopentyl ternary solid solution now gives an 18 K span at just 1 kbar - https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16488 - a 7× to 20× jump over NPG at the same low pressure. That directly attacks the old "needs enormous pressure" objection.
"Parity with existing refrigerator compressors" - Barocal's only public performance claim, with no disclosed number behind it.
We now believe the strong hypothesis is WEAKENED (45%→30%): the material works, but the device does not yet, and the economics do not close. The gap between a good crystal and a good product is the whole story here, and the measured hardware is far behind the material.
Measured caloric devices deliver a 3-4 K temperature span; useful AC/refrigeration needs ~20-40 K. Barocal's 40 K is a funded TARGET, not a built device.
The best measured devices move heat across only 3-4 K, while useful refrigeration needs roughly 20-40 K. The best measured barocaloric prototype delivered a 3.8 K drop and 900 J - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666386424002418; the best measured elastocaloric device, a 2025 beverage-can cooler, reached a 3.5 K span with a COP that was never measured - https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/materials/articles/10.3389/fmats.2025.1563997/full (5.8 was simulated). The seductive efficiencies - barocaloric 7.5-14, Barocal's modelled 5.5-13.1 - https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ente.202401057 - are all material-level or simulation.

Peer review blames the system, not the crystal. Actuators run 30-60% efficient with ~77% work recovery, and nitinol hysteresis of 0.46 J/g is "too large to be competitive with vapour compression" - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955822/. The material's headline entropy is not the constraint; the pumps, seals, fatigue and heat-exchange losses that turn a crystal into a machine are. That is why the measured COP keeps refusing to show up.

Now the gating layer: the whole financial case rests on avoiding an F-gas penalty that barely exists. The EU mandatory HFC quota fee is €3/t CO2e - https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/fluorinated-greenhouse-gases/faq_en; the secondary quota only ~€15.9/t - https://refindustry.com/news/market-research/refrigerant-price-monitoring-q4-2024-data/; the US AIM Act sets no public allowance price at all - https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/frequent-questions-phasedown-hydrofluorocarbons. Meanwhile the incumbent simply switches to propane (R290, GWP 3, SCOP up to 5.66) - https://znfu.com/r290-vs-r32/ or CO2 (R744, GWP 1), keeping COP 4-5 at trivial cost. The cautionary analogues are stark: thermoelectric ships everywhere yet is stuck at COP 0.3-0.7, and magnetocaloric, two decades ahead of caloric, still ships only token magnet-cost-limited coolers - https://medium.com/extantia-capital/reinventing-the-fridge-why-we-invested-in-magnotherm-6efa0ba8b71b.

The moat twist is the sharpest signal in the whole edition: the best-funded team is building the fridge, not licensing the crystal. Barocal sits at TRL 5, raised a $10M seed led by World Fund - https://www.worldfund.vc/articles/barocal-raises-10m-to-disrupt-century-old-cooling-industry-with-breakthrough-materials-technology with Breakthrough Energy Discovery, Cambridge Enterprise Ventures and IP Group's £2m, won a $1M TERA-Award, and holds €2.45M from the EIC to build a 40 K / 1,000 W / 2-3× unit - https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101218476/de. It owns foundational IP (WO2018069506). Yet it is building the full modular AC and refrigeration system - https://barocal.com/about-us/, not licensing the material. If the crystal were a defensible standalone rent, they would license it. Value is migrating from the element to the integration.

The caloric materials are real and improving, but the device economics don't close in 2026-27 and the durable rent is in the integrated system, not the licensable material.
The pitch is "solid-state cooling is the refrigerant-free future - back the breakthrough material." Both halves are premature. The material is real but the device is a 3.5 K lab cooler, and the best-funded team in the world is vertically integrating precisely because the crystal alone won't hold rent. The near-term killer isn't a caloric failure - it's that a $12/lb propane swap hands the incumbent a GWP-3, COP-5 heat pump for almost nothing, so the regulation caloric is betting on never becomes the whip its model needs. This is the third cooling-materials story (after radiative-cooling films and data-centre dielectric fluids) to land on the same law: in passive or bolt-on cooling materials, physics is never the bottleneck, capturability is. Ask who owns the loop before asking whether the device works.

LONG VIEW. The transition's centre of gravity has moved from "can we make clean electrons" to "can we deliver firm, dispatchable power to the compute buildout inside the grid's limits" - and thermal management of that same compute is now its own climate-capital arena. That is the thread this edition pulls.
Caloric cooling sits at the intersection of materials science and the data-centre thermal-management gold rush. Cooling is ~40% of data-centre energy; if any refrigerant-free solid-state approach hit parity at a useful span, it would ride the same AI-power wave funding liquid cooling and dielectric fluids. It doesn't yet - which is why the money in cooling is going to loops, CDUs and low-GWP vapour compression, not caloric materials.
Everyone treats solid-state cooling as a materials bet you win by owning the best crystal. The best-funded team on earth just told you otherwise - Barocal is building the whole fridge, not licensing the material. And the regulation the whole category is waiting for is a €3-a-tonne fee the incumbent sidesteps with a propane refill.
