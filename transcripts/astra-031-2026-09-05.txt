## The Thermal Battery Bet That Just Split in Two
Confidence on this inquiry moved from seventy-one to seventy-four percent today, but the real news is that the single verdict split into two very different bets. Start with the physics, because it is the easy part. A solid-media thermal battery does something almost dumb. It runs electricity through a block of carbon or firebrick, the way a toaster element heats up, storing the energy as heat above one thousand degrees at close to a hundred percent efficiency. The storage medium costs about one dollar per kilowatt-hour of carbon, against three hundred and thirty for lithium. The device is solved.
So the heat case is now near fact. Rondo's hundred megawatt-hour battery at a California oil field, live since October, is charged only by its own on-site solar array in the six cheapest hours of the day, and gives back round-the-clock steam above one thousand degrees at better than ninety-seven percent round trip. That is a fully islanded, unsubsidised, gas-displacing plant, today. Electrified Thermal turned on a twenty megawatt-hour unit in January where the brick itself is the heating element. The heat leg got stronger.
The power leg got weaker, twice over. First, thermophotovoltaics, the panels that would turn that glowing heat back into electricity. The champion single cells hit forty-one to forty-four percent in the lab. But the best efficiency ever measured on an actual module is about eleven percent, and there is no verified figure at all above a hundred kilowatts, let alone a megawatt. Fourth Power's fifty percent number is a target on hardware that will not deliver commercial electricity until around twenty twenty-eight. Second, siting. The whole thesis rested on the idea that you dodge the four-and-a-half-year grid connection queue by going behind the meter. This year that shortcut closed. A December order from the US energy regulator bars big co-located loads from netting their own generation and makes them pay for grid backup anyway. And the queue did not disappear, it moved: Texas alone logged a hundred and ninety-eight gigawatts of large-load connection applications in a single quarter, roughly its entire peak demand. You still interconnect.
Here is the implication, stated plainly. When companies actually buy firm storage for AI data centers, they are not buying thermal batteries. Crusoe is buying Form Energy's iron-air and second-life car batteries. The marquee behind-the-meter power island, Fermi's, is buying gas turbines. Meanwhile Antora just raised five hundred and fifty million dollars at a reported two-point-four-seven billion dollar valuation on a data center narrative, with not one named customer, megawatt, or contract disclosed. The durable, investable thing is narrower and duller than the headline: sell industrial steam in expensive-gas geographies like Europe and India, where the real moat is a bespoke utility tariff, not the physics.

## The Whole Field Is Bending Around the Data Center
The wider field is bending around one force: AI infrastructure. Climate-tech venture funding hit twenty-six billion dollars in the first half, up fifty-five percent, but a third of that went to low-carbon data centers, while carbon-removal equity funding collapsed sixty-one percent. It is a barbell. The shakeout is real: Form Energy, a genuinely strong long-duration storage company, raised a down round, seven hundred and fifty million at a valuation cut from three billion to one-point-seven-five. The Brooklyn electrification pioneer BlocPower simply shut down. Transmission is now the official binding constraint on everything, with the US Department of Energy naming data center load as the driver. And where firm power for data centers is being contracted, it is going to geothermal and nuclear: Fervo signed a three hundred and ninety-six megawatt geothermal deal with Google, with an option to scale toward a gigawatt for a Utah data center. The through-line for the next year or two: capital, firm generation, and above all grid connection decide who actually gets built.
