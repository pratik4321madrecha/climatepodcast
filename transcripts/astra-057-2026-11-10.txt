## The UK deep tech map this week
This week's evidence is thin, and I want to say that plainly. The web search tools returned few dated items for the last few days. What follows is only what was fetched and read this run.

Start in Cambridge, with quantum. On the sixth of November, five universities launched the UK Quantum SuperConnector. They are Cambridge, Imperial, Bristol, Glasgow and Oxford. It is funded by UK Research and Innovation through its quantum programme, though no pound figure was published. It offers entrepreneurship training, investor networks and a national skills centre, so that researchers can turn lab results into companies. Ramana Nanda of Imperial Business School directs it. Aga Iwasiewicz-Wabnig, who runs the Maxwell Centre in Cambridge, is co-director.

A month earlier, on the fifteenth of October, IonQ and the University of Cambridge opened a quantum innovation centre inside the Ray Dolby Centre, the new home of the Cavendish Laboratory. IonQ plans to deploy a machine with two hundred and fifty six qubits there. Nine research projects are underway. Innovate UK is providing three years of access to the National Quantum Computing Centre.

Notice who owns that machine. It is an American company's computer, in a Cambridge building. The Cambridge-born company in this field is Nu Quantum. It closed a sixty million dollar Series A in December 2025, led by National Grid Partners, which is a corporate venture arm, with Gresham House Ventures and Morpheus Ventures taking part. A Spanish state fund put in nine point seven five million euros in January. Nu Quantum builds networking hardware that links quantum processors together, so that several small machines behave like one larger one.

Now London and the rest of the UK. In photonics, Salience Labs, a spinout from Oxford, closed a one hundred and twenty five million dollar Series B in October, with AMD and Intel taking part. It builds optical switches for AI data centres. In climate and energy, Exergy3, from the University of Edinburgh, raised about eleven point four million euros in April to turn surplus renewable power into industrial heat at very high temperature. In AI for materials, CuspAI in Cambridge closed a four hundred and fifty million dollar Series B in July and launched an industry group called the AI Materials Foundry. Nvidia, Meta, Samsung and Applied Materials are named among the founding partners.

How the three connect. Quantum and AI chips both depend on photonics. AI for materials depends on computing power. Climate technology depends on both. The UK is strong at the early, university-born layer. The pattern in these rounds is that the biggest cheques come from corporates and state funds, and the headline hardware often belongs to foreign firms. That is the open question for the next few weeks.

One term worth knowing. Chiplets are small slices of a processor that are made separately and then joined in one package. Cambridge Wireless is running an event on them on the seventeenth of November.

## Who owns UK quantum's commercial layer
We opened a new inquiry this run: is the binding constraint on UK quantum commercialisation access to deployable hardware and customer pull, rather than lab science or capital? We start at fifty five percent, an open verdict, at the first survey depth.

The evidence so far leans toward yes, but it is one afternoon of reading. The state is building convening and access infrastructure. The SuperConnector teaches founders and connects investors. The National Quantum Computing Centre gives time on machines. Cambridge hosts a two hundred and fifty six qubit IonQ system, which is an American company's hardware.

The private money tells a similar story. Nu Quantum's sixty million dollar round was led by a corporate venture arm, with a Spanish state fund taking part. That is a strong round, the largest quantum Series A in the UK according to the reporting, but it is not a pure financial-investor signal.

What would overturn this? Evidence that UK-owned quantum hardware companies are winning paying customers without state access programmes. We did not find that this run, and we did not search for it deeply. That search is the next round. Confidence sits at fifty five percent, and it should be read as a starting point, not a finding.
