The spine · D3 frontier (I ran the numbers)
Start with the physics, because it is the one clause that is genuinely banked: a tandem beats single-junction silicon on the merits, and it is not close. Single-crystal silicon is Auger-limited to about 29.4%. A tandem splits the solar spectrum across a ~1.68 eV perovskite top cell and a 1.12 eV silicon bottom cell so each photon converts with less energy wasted as heat, lifting the theoretical ceiling toward 43%. Certified hardware is already well past silicon: LONGi's 34.85% tandem cell sits comfortably above the silicon Auger wall on NREL's chart - https://www.nrel.gov/pv/cell-efficiency.html, and the 2025 unlock that got it there was a self-assembled-monolayer hole contact that coats textured silicon conformally. The efficiency race is real, it is shipping, and it is almost beside the point.
We went in asking whether tandem has crossed into a bankable commercial product in 2026; we now believe it has not, at 34% confidence, and my own LCOE model pushes the strong "bankable now" form toward overturned. LCOE is the levelized cost of electricity, the all-in cost per unit of power over a plant's life, and it is what a lender actually underwrites. Using NREL's technoeconomic $/W breakpoints for US tandem modules ($0.29 to $0.42/W) - https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/01/09/u-s-made-perovskite-silicon-tandem-solar-modules-could-be-produced-at-around-0-35-w/ against a 2026 TOPCon module near $0.18/W, the answer is uncomfortable for the hype: on open land tandem loses at every efficiency it can ship.

Even at module-$/W parity tandem still loses about 13% on utility LCOE, because the honest incumbent is TOPCon at ~$0.18/W, not the $0.285 PERC benchmark the tandem literature quietly compares itself to. On cheap open land the area-BOS saving from higher efficiency (about $0.05/W) never offsets the module premium (about $0.10/W). The one place the math flips is where area is the binding constraint: on a C&I rooftop, tandem at 30% reaches LCOE parity (+1.1%), and where roof area caps the system, +30% watts per square metre is directly +30% revenue. So what: tandem's first bankable home is area-constrained (C&I rooftop, land-scarce markets, solar-EV, space and defense), never the utility fields the hype implies.
Chart 2: The warranty is the lever. Cutting credited life from 25 to 10 years moves LCOE +140%; a 20% module-price cut moves it -11%; three extra efficiency points move it -2.3%. The binding constraint is an underwriting artefact, not a cost curve.
The single most load-bearing variable in the whole model is credited lifetime, and it swamps everything else by more than ten to one. A bank that can only credit today's 10-year warranty rather than a 25-year life more than doubles the LCOE (+140%). Against that, a 20% module-price cut is worth only -11%, and three extra efficiency points only -2.3%. The market keeps cheering 34% cell records and sub-$0.30/W cost targets while the actual gate is a boring underwriting fact: the length of warranty a lender will credit. That is why bankability here is a durability-certainty problem first and a cost problem a distant second.
This is exactly why the certification dominoes that fell this year have not re-rated the category, they are necessary but not sufficient. The enabling infrastructure arrived on schedule: GCL became the first perovskite module to pass triple-strength IEC 61215/61730 via TUV Rheinland - https://www.perovskite-info.com/gcl-secures-perovskite-module-certification-3x-iec-accelerated-aging-tests, Qcells full-size M10 tandem modules passed IEC/UL UV15/TC200/HF10/DH1000, and ISFH extended ISO/IEC 17025 metrology to G12 tandem cells. But accelerated ageing over roughly 1,000 hours is not a 25-year field warranty, and the three hard gates stayed shut.

The market is conflating three separate things, and the model pulls them apart. Efficiency is banked, the economics and 25-year durability are not, and they are gated by different physics. Three things most observers miss. First, the binding constraint is credited lifetime, an underwriting artefact, not the $/W curve or the next efficiency record, so the only signal that re-rates the category is durability-certainty: a first 20-year warranty, a first PVEL scorecard entry, a first insurer wrap, a first non-recourse loan. Second, even with durability solved, tandem loses on open land at cost parity, so the honest first market is area-constrained rooftop, EV and space, not utility fields. Third, the durable Western wedge is not a module franchise (China takes records, the GW line, the patents, the first orders) but the durability/encapsulation-IP and metrology layer plus a policy-walled US cluster. Deal-sourcing screen: back the encapsulation/metrology IP and the area-constrained-premium plays; the one question to ask any tandem team is which warranty term they can actually finance against, and who insures it.

Ecosystem placement
Tandem sits at the point where two solar truths collide: module efficiency keeps rising while the financing stack, not the cell, decides what gets built. Its real role in the transition is area-constrained generation, squeezing more watts from a fixed roof, vehicle or rooftop where land is the scarce input, not competing with TOPCon on the open fields where cheap silicon already wins on LCOE. That places it alongside the same "own the durable layer, not the commodity" logic seen in cells and storage: China captures the gigawatt module race, and the contestable Western rent moves to encapsulation and metrology IP, jurisdictional manufacturing seats, and the premium niches. It is a product waiting on an underwriting event, not a cost breakthrough.
Everyone is watching the 34% efficiency race and the sub-$0.30/W cost curve. Neither is the bankability gate. Credited lifetime is, and it dominates LCOE about thirteen times more than a big module-price cut. A bank credits the 10-year warranty, not a 25-year life, and that alone more than doubles the LCOE. So the re-rate will be triggered by an unglamorous document (the first 20-year warranty, the first PVEL entry, the first insurer wrap), not a new record. And on open land tandem does not even win at module-$/W parity, because the incumbent is TOPCon at ~$0.18/W, not the $0.285 PERC benchmark the literature quietly compares to.
