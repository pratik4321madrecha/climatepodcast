The spine · run the numbers on caloric cooling
Caloric cooling swaps refrigerant gas for a solid that heats up when you apply a field and cools when you release it, and that entropy swing is the whole cooling cycle: apply the field, shuttle away the heat, release the field, absorb heat from the box you want cold, repeat. There are four modes, named for the field used - barocaloric (pressure, squeezing a plastic crystal like neopentylglycol - https://rmi.org/clean-energy-101-solid-state-cooling/), elastocaloric (mechanical strain on a shape-memory alloy like nitinol/NiTi), electrocaloric (an electric field on a ceramic or polymer), and magnetocaloric (a magnetic field on a metal). It behaves like a bicycle pump warming as you compress it, except the working body is a reusable solid instead of a gas, so there is no high-GWP refrigerant at all (GWP 0). That matters because cooling is roughly 7% of global emissions and the refrigerant gases are a large slice of it.

On the merits the materials are genuinely good and getting better, but the machines lag badly. Neopentylglycol shows an entropy change of ~389 J/kg/K and elastocaloric devices now run at kilowatt scale, so the active material is not the problem. The problem appears the moment you build a real system: actuator and heat-transfer parasitics crush the measured system COP (coefficient of performance - cooling delivered per unit of work in) to 1.2-2.2 at a 20 K lift, against vapour-compression's 3.8-5.2. The materials keep setting records; the delivered machine keeps missing the incumbent.

"The caloric physics is cheap. The engineering is expensive. That is the whole investment case."
The real bankability gate for elastocaloric is not COP, it is cyclic fatigue. A fridge or an AC has to survive 108-109 strain cycles over its life, and until recently the alloys cracked far short of that. Lab records now approach the target: nanocrystalline NiTi tubes past 120 million cycles, and TiNi holding -15.9 K after 10 million cycles - https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68835-0 (Nature Comms, January 2026), plus a Heusler alloy at 1 million cycles and -12.7 K - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1359645425008274 (Acta Materialia, September 2025). But Fraunhofer's demonstrated system-level stability is still 107 - https://www.ipm.fraunhofer.de/en/press-publications/press-releases/elastocaloric-cooling-system-long-term-stability.html. The gap is narrowing fast, and it is a processing and microstructure problem - texture, precipitates, additive manufacturing - which is itself patentable, defensible IP.
Chart 2 - The fatigue gate is closing. Lab coupons now clear 120M cycles; the demonstrated system still sits at 107, orders of magnitude below the 108-109 a real appliance needs.
The market has already priced where the rent is, and it is not the material. Carrier put €15M into elastocaloric Exergyn (Dublin) at a valuation above €200M - https://www.coolingpost.com/world-news/carrier-invests-in-solid-state-heat-pump/ - and it bought equity in the integrated company, not a nitinol licence. MagnoTherm's magnetocaloric Eclipse cabinet, an integrated machine, is shipping into REWE supermarkets at 15% below propane, 10-20 units in 2026 - https://naturalrefrigerants.com/news/magnotherm-rolls-out-refrigerant-free-magnetic-cooling-cabinets-in-rewe-stores/. Cambridge's Barocal (Xavier Moya's spinout, $10M seed May 2026) - https://www.enterprise.cam.ac.uk/news/barocal_raises_10m_cooling_industry/ is building the full system too, and still discloses no COP, no $/kW and no TRL. The near-term financial-first niche is not the loudly-marketed data centre - immersion and direct-to-chip already own that - but refrigerant-free commercial refrigeration and silent, vibration-free point-of-use. The cleanest investable seat is the shovels: nitinol wire and tube melters like Fort Wayne Metals and SAES Smart Materials, who supply every elastocaloric player regardless of which mode wins.
