## Carbon-free aluminium works at commercial scale - but the inert anode may not pay
Inert-anode aluminium: the cell that breathes oxygen, not CO2
It just ran at commercial amperage. The physics that makes it clean also makes it hungry.
The electrochemistry is now proven at commercial scale, and that is exactly what weakens the investment case. ELYSIS ran a 450 kA inert-anode cell in Quebec that emits oxygen instead of the 1,500 kg of direct CO2 a carbon anode releases per tonne of aluminium. But taking away the burning carbon anode also takes away the heat it supplied, so the cell needs about 3 kWh/kg more electricity - a hard physics penalty that makes the technology cleanest on the hydro grids that were already clean and dirtier than carbon on the coal grids that emit most. On a clean hydro grid the inert anode wipes out the direct process CO2. On a coal grid the extra electricity it draws emits more than the carbon it removed, so total CO2 goes up. That inversion is the whole edition in one chart.
An inert anode replaces the carbon block that is burned away in every aluminium cell with an oxygen-evolving electrode that is not consumed, so the cell breathes out oxygen instead of carbon dioxide. Every tonne of aluminium is made by Hall-Heroult smelting ("running electric current through alumina dissolved in molten cryolite salt at about 960C to split out the metal"). The catch is the anode: it is made of carbon, and the oxygen freed from the alumina attacks it, burning 400-450 kg of carbon per tonne of metal into roughly 1,500 kg of direct CO2 for every tonne of aluminium - https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/clean-aluminum/alcoa-riotinto-hit-low-carbon-production-milestone. Swap that consumable carbon for a metal-or-ceramic anode the oxygen cannot burn, and the direct process emissions go to zero.
The reason this took 130 years is not the electrochemistry but the materials: nothing survives long in molten fluoride at 960C. The anode sits in a bath as corrosive as it is hot, and the oxygen it evolves is the most aggressive part. A cermet or nickel-iron-copper alloy anode has to conduct current, evolve oxygen, and resist dissolution all at once, which is why the definitive 2025 review concludes that a truly inert anode is unlikely to be found - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2468519425006998 and the real target is one that corrodes slowly enough to be economic. It is the same problem a spark plug faces, scaled to a swimming pool of molten salt: the electrode has to keep working in the exact conditions designed to destroy it.
The "does it work" question is now settled: the cell runs at commercial current and makes commercial-grade metal. In November 2025 ELYSIS, the Rio Tinto and Alcoa joint venture, started up a 450 kA commercial-size inert-anode cell - https://www.riotinto.com/en/news/releases/2025/elysis-achieves-breakthrough-with-commercial-size-cell at the Alma smelter in Quebec, producing 99.7% purity P1020 aluminium with zero direct smelting CO2. The amperage curve tells the scaling story: 100 kA in 2021 to 450 kA in 2025. Apple has already taken a first commercial carbon-free batch - https://elysis.com/en/apple-purchases-from-elysis-a-first-carbon-free-commercial-aluminium-batch. After Alcoa killed a $100M inert-anode program in the 1980s on durability, that is a genuine milestone.
Amperage climbed from 100 kA to 450 kA in four years and CEO Francois Perras targets commercial maturity around 2030. The 2026-28 diligence window is where the one load-bearing number, an independent at-amperage opex and anode-wear dataset, is still missing.
Verdict · WEAKENED · 50% → 38%The technology works on the merits at 450 kA. The strong-form thesis, that a rational smelter buys inert anodes for lower opex rather than a green premium, is not yet evidenced: the merchant case rests on the JV's own unaudited numbers against an independent physics penalty of about 3 kWh/kg. A 2030 incumbent optionality play, not a merchant upgrade today.
The gate the milestone hides is the whole-cell energy balance, and the physics runs the opposite way to the marketing. When the carbon anode burns, that combustion supplies heat and stirs the bath. Take it away and you have to put that energy back as electricity: SINTEF's Asbjorn Solheim estimates an extra 3 kWh/kg, about one added volt - https://blog.sintef.com/energy/aluminium-electrolysis-using-inert-anodes/, lifting the theoretical minimum from 6.24 to 9.16 kWh/kg. That penalty is independent physics, not a vendor estimate. So what: on any grid where a kilowatt-hour carries carbon, the inert cell hands some of its CO2 saving straight back through the meter.

This flips which smelters the technology actually helps, and it is the reverse of where decarbonisation is most needed. The inert anode is cleanest where power is cleanest and cheapest, the hydro grids of Quebec, Norway and Russia, and worst on the coal grids of India and China that make the dirty tonnes. On coal power the added electricity can push total CO2 above what the carbon anode emitted - https://blog.sintef.com/energy/aluminium-electrolysis-using-inert-anodes/. The merchant case leans entirely on numbers the joint venture has not let anyone check. The JV cites 15% lower opex, 15% higher productivity and 30x anode life, all unaudited. Against that sits a thin green premium of about $100-200 per tonne on a roughly $3,150 per tonne LME base, per the 2026 WEF low-carbon metals economics - https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_The_Economics_of_Low-Carbon_Metals_2026.pdf. The flagship demand is brand-and-subsidy: Apple plus roughly $144M of Rio, Alcoa, Apple, Canada and Quebec money. No smelter has committed a merchant line above 100 kt/yr on cost terms. So what: without the independent opex teardown, the whole case is policy-dependent, not merchant.
The other actors reveal the real read by what they are choosing instead. RUSAL claims the first industrial inert-anode metal on hydropower - https://www.lightmetalage.com/news/industry-news/smelting/rusal-produces-low-carbon-aluminum-using-inert-anode-technology/. Norsk Hydro's HalZero, a different chloride chemistry, claims only cost parity - https://www.hydro.com/en/global/sustainability/our-roadmap-to-net-zero-emissions-in-aluminium-production/halzero-zero-emission-electrolysis-from-hydro, not an advantage. And EGA, sitting on cheap Gulf power, is decarbonising via nuclear, solar and carbon capture rather than inert anodes at all - https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/uae-collaboration-delivers-first-low-carbon-aluminium. When the cheapest-power smelter in the world skips the technology, the merchant thesis has a problem.

The deeper reason this is a decade-out incumbent play is that it is not a part swap but a full cell redesign. Perras is explicit that an inert-anode cell looks more like a battery than a potline - https://magazine.cim.org/en/news/2025/elysis-achieves-commercial-size-cell-milestone-en/, with commercial maturity around 2030. That is why the value is owned inside Rio Tinto, Alcoa, Hydro and RUSAL, and why a startup cannot enter at the smelter. So what: the tradeable, near-term edge is the enabling materials layer, corrosion-resistant alloy anodes, wettable cathodes and molten-salt corrosion sensing, where a small science team could supply every smelter regardless of who owns the pot.
Most observers read the 450 kA cell as "carbon-free aluminium has arrived." The sharper read is that a clean cell running on a dirty grid can emit more than the process it replaced, so this technology decarbonises last where decarbonisation is needed most. As a sourcing filter: do not chase the smelter, it is incumbent-owned and a decade out. Back the materials-durability layer that every route needs, and set one trigger to re-rate the whole category - the first independent, at-amperage, full-year opex and anode-wear dataset. Until that number exists, treat every opex claim as marketing.

Placed on the whole transition, inert-anode aluminium is the same shape as green hydrogen: a proven principle whose economics are broken by a physics penalty, not a concept problem. Hydrogen stalls on the energy cost of electrolysis; the inert anode stalls on the extra 3 kWh/kg; both are cleanest exactly where clean power is already cheap and abundant. The transition's binding constraint has moved from cost of the device to the whole-system energy balance around it, and the capital that reads that layer early is the capital that is not overpaying for the milestone.
