The spine · how the cell actually works
An inert anode replaces the burning carbon block in an aluminium cell with a metal or cermet block that evolves oxygen instead of carbon dioxide. In a conventional Hall-Heroult cell - https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/ace332 (the only industrial route to primary aluminium), electric current runs through alumina dissolved in molten cryolite at about 960°C, and the carbon anode is consumed to CO2 at roughly 1.5 t of direct CO2 per tonne of aluminium, so it must be replaced constantly. Swap in an anode that does not burn and two things happen at once: you stop buying and replacing carbon, and you stop the process CO2. It turns the cell more like a battery than a potline, which is why it is a full cell redesign and not a part-swap.
450 kA commercial-size cell running at Rio Tinto's Alma smelter, Quebec, since Nov 2025, at P1020 (99.7%) purity. "Does it work" is settled.
The reframe is that we actually ran the breakeven, and the anode-cost saving beats the energy penalty before any green premium. With LME aluminium at $3,177/t on 30 July 2026, the inert cell carries an energy penalty of about +3 kWh/kg per SINTEF's Solheim - https://blog.sintef.com/energy/aluminium-electrolysis-using-inert-anodes/, which is 3 MWh/t, so its cost is 3 times the power price. Against that, not buying consumable carbon anodes saves $150-300/t. At a hydro power price at or below $50/MWh, or with a modest carbon price, the opex line alone can come out ahead. That is not what a dead thesis looks like, and it is the reason confidence did not collapse to zero.
The productivity gate: independent, peer-reviewed inert-anode cells run below carbon's 93-96% current efficiency. Fewer tonnes per cell-year raises capex per annual tonne, which the opex-only breakeven never captures.
The breakeven omits the two decisive numbers, and both push the true crossover left toward cheap clean power plus a carbon price. First, current efficiency (the fraction of the current that actually deposits metal rather than doing nothing useful): independent peer-reviewed cells run 60-91% against carbon's 93-96%, so each cell makes fewer tonnes per year and the capital cost per annual tonne rises. Second, the real anode-replacement cost and cell life at commercial amperage, which nobody has published. Put both back in and the profitable corner shrinks back to a policy-and-premium story, not an opex upgrade.
"When the one number that would prove your cost advantage is exactly the number you refuse to publish, the silence is the finding."
The tell · INQ-020
The clinching tell is that ELYSIS has run the 450 kA cell for about eight months and discloses neither a current-efficiency nor a kWh/kg figure. A firm sitting on a genuine opex win publishes the number that proves it, because that number is the whole sales pitch to every smelter on Earth. Instead the announcement - https://elysis.com/en/elysis-achieves-breakthrough-with-commercial-size-cell-a-first-in-aluminium-production-using-the leads with purity and cell size and stays quiet on efficiency. The opacity is not an accident of PR; it is the signal that the productivity number is not yet a competitive one.

Three hardening facts turn the merchant thesis into its opposite. The purity cap is structural: metallic inert-anode metal is 99.4-99.7% (Fe 0.25-1.0 wt%, Ni 0.13 wt%), and RUSAL's "Inerta" - https://www.alcircle.com/press-release/rusal-produces-high-purity-aluminium-with-breakthrough-inert-anode-technology-115272 has made roughly 6,000 t at A7/P1020 since 2017, which excludes the 99.9%-plus aerospace grades, so its only real premium is a green brand premium of about $100-200/t on hydro metal, not purity and not cost. That is the opposite of the hypothesis: a green premium, not a financial upgrade.

Revealed preference confirms it: ELYSIS's first and only smelter licence is captive, Rio Tinto to Rio Tinto. The Arvida project - https://www.aluminium-journal.com/elysis-issues-its-first-smelter-technology-licence is a 100 kA, ten-pot line with first metal in 2027 and Alcoa taking up to 40% offtake, and every named offtaker (Apple, AB InBev, Audi, Ball) is buying a premium brand, not validating opex. No third party has run the cell and confirmed the economics. Meanwhile the independent pure-play VSCA is Norwegian, founder-funded and pre-institutional, with no round, no Horizon grant and no customer, and Trimet in Essen - https://www.trimet.eu/en/trimet/sustainability/environmental-and-climate-protection/production-of-inert-metallic-anodes runs a second 10-to-40 kA metallic-anode pilot on German BMWK and EU money, which is policy-first and sub-scale.
So what we now believe: the technology works, but the merchant case is a policy and brand story, further weakened, because inert anodes decarbonise the already-clean smelters and barely touch the coal tonnes that emit most. A rational major such as EGA buys cleaner power, not inert anodes, because that is where its emissions actually sit. The tradeable near-term edge is the enabling-materials and measurement layer under the smelter, not the smelter itself, and this is the sixth instance of value migrating from the element to integration, qualification and measurement. Anyone underwriting inert-anode smelting as a 2026-2030 opex upgrade is pricing an unproven productivity number, and the settling event is the Arvida 100 kA current-efficiency dataset in 2027 - https://www.aluminium-journal.com/elysis-issues-its-first-smelter-technology-licence plus the first non-captive merchant licence.

Most observers read the ELYSIS 450 kA milestone as proof the economics have arrived; they have confused a working device with a working business. The device is real and the purity is real, but the two numbers that decide whether a smelter buys it for money, current efficiency and anode life at amperage, are exactly the two the venture withholds. The sourcing filter: ignore the cell, price the measurement and materials layer beneath it, because the artifact nobody owns yet is an independent, at-amperage anode-wear and current-efficiency dataset that every diligence desk needs.

The long view
Demand for 24/7 clean power to feed AI compute is now the transition's primary financing driver, pulling capital to dispatchable heat and power and away from subsidy-dependent US wind and solar as OBBBA credits sunset. The durable edge over 12-36 months is AI-materials-discovery paired with real industrial offtake, the Antora and CuspAI template.
Ecosystem placement
Inert-anode aluminium sits in the same family as every other "electrify the molecule" bet, and it maps onto the transition as a clean-power problem wearing a chemistry costume. Its emissions win is real only where power is already clean, which is why it competes for capital against Boston Metal's molten oxide electrolysis for steel, against green hydrogen DRI, and against the heat-battery rounds above, all of which convert cheap firm electricity into a decarbonised industrial product. The system lesson is consistent: the metal or molecule is the commodity, and the defensible value keeps migrating to the electrodes, the sensors and the qualification data that decide whether the cheap-power conversion actually holds up in a real plant.
Contrarian take
The consensus treats the missing efficiency number as a disclosure gap that will close with time; read it instead as the answer. If ELYSIS could publish a current efficiency at or above carbon's 93-96% at 450 kA, it would have, on day one, in the headline, because that single figure would sell licences to every smelter on the planet. Eight months of silence is not caution, it is a number that is not ready. The bull case is not "the data will come"; the bull case has already been priced by the absence of the data.
