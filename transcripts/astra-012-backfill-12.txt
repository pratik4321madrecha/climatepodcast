The China-free premium is at a 2026 high going into the truce cliff: NdPr alloy ran from about $53/kg in January to $133/kg on 1 July, up more than 100% year-to-date. This is the single input the strong thesis needed, handed to it at full strength.
A permanent magnet has to do two physical jobs at once, and iron-nitride can only do one of them well. It must store field (its maximum energy product, MGOe, "how much magnetic work a kilogram holds") and it must resist demagnetising when hot (its coercivity, in oersteds). The best sintered neodymium-iron-boron (NdFeB) does both. Niron's - https://www.nironmagnetics.com/ iron-nitride (Fe16N2) delivers about 10 MGOe, ~0.9 T remanence and 4,000-5,000 Oe coercivity - https://www.emobility-engineering.com/clean-earth-iron-nitride-magnets/: the physics of a ferrite or bonded magnet, not a traction magnet. The plain analogy is that it is a genuinely good speaker or sensor magnet and a physical non-starter as a motor magnet. This is a materials ceiling set by a weak magnetic anisotropy field, not a funding gap a bigger round would close.
Iron-nitride is capped in the bonded band. It sits with bonded NdFeB and above ferrite, but the traction rotor of an EV motor is served near 45 MGOe by sintered NdFeB. A record premium cannot buy performance the material cannot physically deliver.
We went in asking whether the China supply premium makes a good-enough, China-free magnet a financial-first buy for high-performance motors; we now believe it does not, through 2028, at lower confidence (30% to 24%), because a supply-shock premium is a demand pull for performance-certain tons that a physics-capped magnet cannot supply. The refined claim survives and hardens: iron-nitride banks first as a rare-earth-free ferrite, AlNiCo and bonded-NdFeB replacement (audio now shipping, then sensors, appliances, small and auxiliary motors), where its low-but-stable magnetics are a feature. The strong claim, an NdFeB-replacement traction magnet riding the premium, is the one that died.
July was the decisive experiment, because it handed the strong thesis its best-case input and the answer still came back no. The premium widened to a 2026 high (NdPr $133/kg, +21.4% month-on-month - https://www.mainrichinternational.com/resources/articles/rare-earth-market-update-july-2026-ndpr-mofcom-western-supply), China-to-US magnet exports ran 20% below last year despite the trade truce, and Beijing kept escalating enforcement (MOFCOM added MP and USA Rare Earth to its control list on 22 June and created a criminal whistleblower regime on 1 July - https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/recent-china-export-control-actions-signal-active-enforcement-for-rare-earths-and-strategic-minerals), all pointing at the 10 November truce cliff. If any premium was ever going to reach rare-earth-free chemistry, this was the moment it would show up in a contract.

The traction slot is being filled without iron-nitride from two directions at once, so the substitute is squeezed above and to the side. From above, Proterial's de-dysprosium/terbium NdFeB reaches 1.42 T - https://www.proterial.com/e/press/2025/n0722b.html and removes the heavy-rare-earth chokepoint while keeping full performance. From the side, the OEM route increasingly deletes the magnet entirely: Renault and Valeo target a wound-field traction drive for 2027, and India's VIMAG Labs closed a $5M Series A for magnet-free motors on 13 July - https://electrek.co/2026/07/13/vimag-labs-magnet-free-ev-motor-patent/. And within its own band, Nichia's anisotropic SmFeN already ships China-free at 14-18 MGOe, above iron-nitride. The most under-priced threat to a rare-earth-free magnet is not a Chinese magnet, it is a motor with no magnet at all.

In any supply-shock story, the sourcing filter is not the size of the premium, it is which chemistry is signing binding tons. When the incumbent takes take-or-pay and the challenger takes equity, the challenger is priced as an option, and you underwrite it as one. For rare-earth-free magnets specifically: do not back iron-nitride as a 2026-28 traction bet. Back it, if at all, as an audio, sensor and auxiliary-motor product plus a 2028 gigafactory scale bet, and put the bankable-now capital into China-free NdFeB and recycling. The re-rate trigger is unglamorous: a disclosed Gen-1 $/kg below bonded NdFeB, or a qualified motor design-in with a start-of-production date, or a pre-2028 offtake that is tons rather than equity.

Upstream cell science kept compounding under the noise. A silicon-anode binder held an Ah-level pouch cell above 2,000 cycles at 99.93% average Coulombic efficiency - https://www.nature.com/nenergy/research-articles, and dual-atom catalysts for Li-S and Li-air reframed catalyst design around solid-phase electron transport. This is the next-generation cell IP that decides which storage chemistries bank in the 2030s.
The long view: over 12-36 months AI power demand, not decarbonisation policy, is the dominant force setting climate-tech capital allocation, pulling money into fusion, deep geothermal, firm nuclear, storage and grid reform while starving low-carbon fuels and early-stage. The winners pencil on raw economics plus a hyperscaler offtake, not subsidy, and the UK carves a fusion-services and critical-minerals-processing niche even as US and EU capital leads.
Placed on the whole transition, magnets are the same story as sodium storage and green hydrogen: technical merit is real, and value migrates off the headline element to durability, integration and Western manufacturing. The energy transition needs magnets for EV traction, wind turbines and robotics, so the critical-minerals layer is the supply-security floor under all three. But the rent does not sit in the novel chemistry; it sits in whoever can ship performance-certain tons under contract, which today means recycled and mined NdFeB plus the process IP to make it China-free. The capital that reads that one layer past the headline metric is the capital that is not overpaying for a supply scare.
Contrarian Take
The "rare-earth crisis" trade has almost nothing to do with rare-earth-FREE magnets. The money flows exactly where the physics says: to China-free NdFeB, recycled by Noveon and mined by MP under a government price floor, in take-or-pay tons, while the challenger chemistry raises options-premium equity and ships speakers. Iron-nitride never used dysprosium or terbium, so the heavy-rare-earth premium it is sold on was never its cost to save. And the biggest threat to a rare-earth-free magnet is not a Chinese magnet, it is a motor with no magnet at all.
