The spine · workshop
The product is a wire whose core barely expands when it heats
An advanced conductor (the ACCC class) replaces a transmission line's steel core with a carbon-fibre composite core whose coefficient of thermal expansion is about 14x lower than aluminium (1.61e-6 vs 23e-6 per degC). Steel-cored wire sags into trees when it heats, so grid operators cap it near 75C. Swap in the composite core and the same span holds its shape at 180-200C, which lets you pack in about 28% more aluminium and push roughly 2x the current at 25-40% lower resistive loss (GridLab technical report - https://www.2035report.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/GridLab_2035-Reconductoring-Technical-Report.pdf). Think of it as swapping a rope that stretches in the sun for one that does not: same poles, same right-of-way, far more current before it droops.
Reconductoring is the operation of doing exactly that on an existing line, restringing the towers you already own. The number that matters is ampacity (the maximum current a conductor can carry continuously before it overheats and sags past code). Two field jobs settle whether the physics survives contact with a real tower.

Then the number that shrinks the prize. The interconnection queue (the backlog of generation and load projects waiting for permission to connect to the grid) sits near 2,060 GW in the US (LBNL Queued Up - https://emp.lbl.gov/queues). Reconductoring only clears the projects held up by a hot, sagging wire. Split the queue and only about 50-65% is that thermally limited share. The other 20-30% is gated by substations and the large power transformer (the multi-tonne substation unit that steps voltage up and down; each one is bespoke), and those carry 3-5 year lead times that a new wire cannot shorten (Power Magazine - https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/).
Chart 2. Of the ~2,060 GW US queue, roughly 50-65% is thermal-limited and therefore reconductoring-addressable; the 20-30% that is substation and transformer limited (3-5 yr lead) re-binds much of what the wire unlocks. About 30-50% of data-centre sites sit behind that transformer gate.
The addressable half is still under-built for a regulatory reason, not a physics one. Under cost-of-service (capex-bias) regulation a utility earns a set return on the capital it deploys, so a cheaper upgrade earns it less money. Restringing a line for a fraction of a new build is a smaller rate base and a smaller profit, which is why the faster, cheaper option keeps losing to the expensive one on the operator's own spreadsheet. That is the whole gate.
The fix that works is debt and permitting, not a fatter return. The obvious repair, paying utilities a higher return for advanced conductors, has existed federally since the 2005 Energy Policy Act as a 200-basis-point adder, and it drew zero uptake because a return premium invites the prudence scrutiny utilities would rather avoid. What is actually moving steel is DOE debt to the regulated owner, an AEP $1.6B loan in Oct-25 - https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/aep-1-6-billion-transmission-project-closes-the-trump-does-first-loan/ for 5,000 miles and an up-to-$3.26B loan to AEP Texas on 8-Jul-26 - https://www.utilitydive.com/news/doe-closes-up-to-326b-loan-to-aep-texas/824802/, plus state expedited-permitting like New Jersey's Advanced Grid Technologies Act (signed 9-Jul-26, 120-day review) - https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-jersey-transmission-oversight-data-center-affordability-sherrill/824681/.
The load-bearing open item is a list DOE has not yet published. Its $1.9B SPARK program - https://www.energy.gov/oe/speed-power-through-accelerated-reconductoring-and-other-key-advanced-transmission-technology remains unawarded, with selections "Anticipated Aug 2026." Whether it funds reconductoring at scale is the single datapoint that would move this verdict again.

VERDICT: CONFIRMED
80% → 82%. The product works and wins on the merits where the limit is thermal. The gating factor is a rate formula and a transformer queue, not the wire.
The value migrated from the element to the integration layer. Again.
This is the seventh instance of the same pattern we keep seeing in deep tech: the differentiated component becomes a commodity and the rent moves to qualification, testing and deployment. The composite core is already there. The flagship patent (EP1506085) was revoked, Exel co-authored the non-exclusive IEC 62818 qualification spec - https://exelcomposites.com/composite-conductor-cores/ and signed a EUR22M / 4-year Tratos supply deal - https://interplasinsights.com/plastics-materials/latest-plastics-materials-news/exel-composites-tratos-four-year-composite-conductor-core-supply/, and TS Conductor raised $85.5M and opened a $134M plant - https://www.compositesworld.com/news/ts-conductor-opens-134-million-south-carolina-facility-for-aecc-conductor-cores.
ACCC cores are brittle, the core snaps first under install and radial stress, which makes installation the safety-critical and therefore rent-bearing step. Deal-sourcing filter: back the regulated asset owner, the qualification/testing/installation layer, and grid software. Never a composite-core startup.

Long view
Over the next 12-36 months capital consolidates around firm, dispatchable clean power for compute (nuclear, fusion, geothermal, multi-day storage) while merchant hydrogen and offtake-less US manufacturing keep failing. The grid itself, the wires and the transformers that carry those electrons, is the bottleneck that decides how much of the queue ever connects, and it is exactly where today's finding sits.
Where this sits in the whole system: every arc above assumes the electrons can reach the load, and reconductoring is the supply-side lever that decides whether the AI-power gravity well actually gets fed. Generation is no longer the binding constraint; interconnection is. Restringing existing towers is the fastest way to widen the pipe for the thermal-limited half of the queue, which makes it the physical complement to the storage cost-curve race and the mirror image of demand-flexibility, the two other ways to relieve the same squeeze.
Contrarian take
Everyone frames the grid bottleneck as "we need to build," and the sophisticated version is "reconductoring solves it cheaply." Both are half-wrong. Only about half the near-term capacity can come from restringing existing lines, because the other half is gated by transformer lead times a wire cannot touch.
The sharpest edge: the "obvious fix," a higher return for advanced conductors, has been on the federal books since 2005, and nobody used it, because a return premium invites prudence scrutiny. The money is moving instead through DOE loans and permitting statutes, straight to the regulated owner and the incumbent wire makers, never to a composite-core moat that has already been revoked in court.
