The gate is a rate formula, and it is more stubborn than it looks
The binding constraint is regulatory capex-bias, not physics or bankability.
A regulated utility earns its allowed return on rate base (the capital on which it is permitted to earn), so a cheaper upgrade earns it less than an expensive new line, which is a direct incentive to over-build. The fix everyone cites is thinner than advertised: Montana's law grants only

Long view.
AI power demand is now the decisive force, and the next phase is delivering firm clean power fast. The cheap, geopolitically-safe layer, LFP and sodium storage, ex-China tandem PV, grid-AI and reconductoring, compounds; the expensive subsidy-dependent layer, green-hydrogen steel and unpriced carbon removal, consolidates around captive offtakers. The UK and Cambridge openings sit in grid-AI software and the ex-China supply build-out, not in new hardware chemistry.
Ecosystem Placement
Reconductoring is the fast, cheap delivery layer of the energy transition, and in 2026 delivery is the binding constraint, not generation.
Solar, wind and storage are already cheaper than the fossil incumbents at the device level; what stalls is connecting them to load through a grid that was built for a smaller, steadier system. Reconductoring sits alongside storage and dynamic line rating as the tools that squeeze more usable capacity out of existing corridors, while new lines and transformers remain the slow, expensive backstop. It is the supply-side complement to making AI load itself flexible: relieve the thermal limit with better wire, shave the peak with smarter demand, and the queue clears years faster than a build-only strategy allows.
Contrarian take
Everyone frames the grid bottleneck as "we need to build." The evidence says close to 90% of the near-term interzonal capacity the US needs by 2035 can come from re-stringing lines that already exist, at under half the cost and a third of the time, and the reason it is not happening is a rate formula that rewards the expensive option. The sharper edge: the "obvious fix" people point to, Montana's advanced-conductor law, does not even do what it is credited with, so the mispricing is not about to self-correct. The money will flow, but to the regulated asset owner and the incumbent wire makers, never to a composite-core moat that has already been revoked in court.
