## The UK Deep-Tech Week: CuspAI's Foundry, the Quantum Money, and the Wires Between Chips
Start with the single biggest UK move of the season. CuspAI, the Cambridge company that uses artificial intelligence to design new materials before anyone makes them, raised four hundred and fifty million dollars in July at a valuation near two and a half billion, and launched what it calls an AI Materials Foundry, a consortium of more than forty-five members including Nvidia, Samsung and Applied Materials pooling computing power and lab access. That is the pattern for the whole sector. AI for science only exists because of large national computers, which is why the UK Sovereign AI fund keeps appearing on these cap tables, and why Isambard-AI in Bristol, the country's most powerful supercomputer with more than five thousand Nvidia superchips, matters as infrastructure the whole field leans on.
In quantum and chips, Cambridge holds the error-correction layer through Riverlane, and the trapped-ion lineage through Quantinuum, formerly Cambridge Quantum. London adds silicon-based qubits through Quantum Motion out of University College London. The defining fact of the year, though, is an exit: Oxford Ionics was bought by the American company IonQ for about one billion dollars, which tells you where UK quantum value tends to end up, in a US acquirer's hands.
In climate and energy, Cambridge has Nyobolt, the fast-charging battery company now aiming at data-centre power, and Carbon13, the venture builder that has helped start more than ninety climate companies and is the clearest on-ramp for a non-technical founder. London's defining story is Octopus Energy spinning out its Kraken software platform at a valuation near nine billion dollars, which is really an artificial-intelligence company wearing an energy badge.
The map in one line: Britain is world-class at the science and the algorithms, and at convening early money around them, but thin on the downstream it does not own, sovereign fabrication and at-scale manufacturing, which is exactly where the durable margin lands.
Three terms to carry into the room this week. First, atomistic foundation model: a single pre-trained model that predicts the energy and forces of almost any arrangement of atoms, so one model generalises across molecules and crystals instead of needing a bespoke one each time. It is the engine under CuspAI-style design. Second, dual-rail erasure qubit: a qubit encoded so that its most common error throws the state into a detectable flag rather than silently corrupting the answer, which makes error correction much cheaper because you know which qubit failed and when. Third, virtual power plant: software that aggregates thousands of home batteries, electric cars and heat pumps and dispatches them as if they were one power station. That is the business model under Kraken.

## Where UK Photonics Actually Makes Money
The verdict split in two directions today. The strong claim, that Britain will capture the rent in optical interconnect through a materials advantage, weakened from fifty to thirty percent. The refined claim, that the money is real but the rent lands elsewhere, firmed to about seventy-two percent. Here is the mechanism. Co-packaged optics, which means moving the light-handling parts directly onto the switch package instead of using pluggable modules, is now shipping. Nvidia's Spectrum-X Photonics reaches general availability in the second half of this year at up to four hundred terabits per second with roughly five times better power efficiency. The device-level energy math is real, about six point two five picojoules per bit for co-packaged optics against seventeen and a half for a pluggable. So a rational data-centre buyer, who does not care about climate at all, buys this on power and bandwidth. That is the market half, and it is confirmed. Capital agrees: Ayar Labs pulled in six hundred and fifty million dollars this year, and Marvell agreed to buy Celestial AI for between three and a quarter and five and a half billion, the first big interconnect exit.
What fails is the idea that the UK owns the defensible part. The durable rent sits at the scale-packaging and foundry layer, with Taiwan's TSMC, with SMART Photonics and GlobalFoundries opening a shared indium-phosphide foundry, and with Tower Semiconductor, which is the company actually manufacturing the UK startup Salience Labs' optical switches. Britain's genuine seat is upstream: open prototyping foundries like CORNERSTONE at Southampton, and early optical-computing bets out of Oxford. The investable lesson for a non-technical operator is specific. Do not try to out-build TSMC. Own the commercial layer, the design-in management, the qualification, the offtake, around a foundry you partner rather than own. That is the twenty-sixth time this engine has watched value migrate off the clever component and onto the integration and offtake layer.

## Checking Every Box Still Isn't A Business
The wider field this fortnight told one connected story: cost moats beat thesis moats. The clearest negative signal came from Ascend Elements, a US battery recycler once named among the most innovative companies, which filed for bankruptcy after a federal grant was cancelled and lithium prices collapsed, having raised more than five hundred million dollars. Domestic-manufacturing and circular-economy credentials did not survive a commodity-price crash. Against that, storage economics decoupled from the rest of clean power: a four-hour battery project fell twenty-seven percent in a year to a record low of seventy-eight dollars per megawatt-hour, even as solar and wind costs ticked up. The cost story has flipped from making energy to firming it. On the science side, a Nature paper reported large-area perovskite solar modules that passed standard reliability testing at higher yield than commercial silicon, which matters because durability, not efficiency, was always perovskite's blocker. And Europe blinked, moving to postpone its methane-emissions law under pressure. The long view: the transition's centre of gravity is shifting to moving energy and moving data, both pulled by AI demand, and the winners sell into that demand on pure cost, climate-positive almost by accident.
