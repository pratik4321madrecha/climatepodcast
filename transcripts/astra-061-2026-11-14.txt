## Cambridge Backs Physics AI, Photonics Takes the Biggest Cheque
The week in UK deep tech is about who writes the cheques. Start in AI for science and materials. Cambridge Innovation Capital, the university's spinout fund, led an eight point four million euro seed extension in BeyondMath, a London company building a physics simulation model it says speeds up engineering simulation by up to a thousand times. That is the company's own claim. Cambridge founders are also on the compute side. Callosum, started by two Cambridge PhDs, raised a one hundred million dollar seed in August, with the UK Sovereign AI fund in the round. It builds software that sends each part of an AI job to the chip and model that suits it. That idea has a name, heterogeneous compute, meaning many kinds of chip in one system, with a routing layer deciding which part of the job goes where.

In quantum, the state is acting as a buyer. On the sixth of November the National Quantum Computing Centre signed a licence for UK organisations to use Phasecraft's software, for materials and chemistry problems among others. Expressions of interest open in late November and the first group starts in spring 2027. One term from that story: resource estimation. It means working out how many qubits and operations a problem needs on an error-corrected machine, so you know whether it is reachable this decade.

In photonics, Oxford's Salience Labs closed a one hundred and twenty five million dollar Series B, led by Bullhound Capital, for a sixty four port all-optical switch for AI data centres. Cambridge Innovation Capital is an existing shareholder. It is the biggest round in this set.

In climate and energy, the money is smaller and the buyer is the state. Imperial spinout Certain Energy raised ten million pounds for a manganese flow battery, led by the British Business Bank with Centrica and Ceres Power in the round. Carbon13 in Cambridge launched its tenth startup fund in June. And Ofgem has proposed a commitment fee on large data centres holding a grid connection. The term for it is credit security: money posted so the grid operator keeps your slot.

The three sectors meet at the data centre. The UK is strong where a university supplies the science and a state buyer writes the first cheque. It is loud where valuations rest on figures that outlets report differently, as with CuspAI, and where hardware exits go abroad.

## Ofgem Wants to Put a Price on a UK Data-Centre Grid Slot
Confidence in the data-centre power inquiry moved from seventy six to seventy eight percent. The thesis is that grid connection rules, not electricity supply, decide which UK AI data centres get built. This session read the rulebook rather than the physics.

Ofgem's consultation of the twenty ninth of July proposes a commitment fee on data centres over forty megawatts. The range is two hundred and thirty seven thousand five hundred to seven hundred and twelve thousand five hundred pounds per megawatt, held as credit security until the site is switched on. By my arithmetic that implies about nine and a half million pounds of capital cost per megawatt. For a hundred megawatt site, that ties up twenty three point seven five to seventy one point two five million pounds.

The fee comes with milestones. A site must show it has chosen to operate or lease, ordered long-lead transformers or switchgear, and proven financial and technical capability. The last milestone asks for compute offtaker commitments, meaning a customer who has agreed to rent the computing. Options under consideration are fifty percent, twenty percent or no minimum. Projects within six months of power, or already past the milestones, look exempt. A decision is expected by the end of 2026.

The case against: this is a proposal, and the fee is small next to a hyperscaler's budget, so it may only deter small speculators. I read a law firm's summary, not Ofgem's own document. If it goes ahead, the scarce asset is a connected site with a tenant, and the investable layers are the ones around it: power flexibility, fast batteries and optical switching.
