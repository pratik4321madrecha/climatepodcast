The spine · workshop (mechanics deep-dive)
A permanent magnet is judged on two numbers, and rare-earth-free chemistry loses the second one exactly where a car needs it. Energy product (BHmax, the magnetic energy a material stores per unit volume, measured in mega-gauss-oersteds, MGOe) sets how much torque a given lump of magnet delivers; coercivity (the reverse magnetic field it takes to demagnetise it, in kilo-oersteds, kOe) sets whether it keeps that torque when the rotor gets hot. A traction motor rotor runs at 150-180C and briefly higher under fault currents - https://chargedevs.com/features/how-to-ensure-ev-traction-motor-magnets-arent-pushed-beyond-their-operating-limits/, and heat erodes coercivity, so the incumbent, sintered NdFeB (neodymium-iron-boron) doped with the heavy rare earths dysprosium and terbium - https://spectrum.ieee.org/ev-motor, exists specifically because Dy and Tb are what hold the field at those temperatures. Think of coercivity as the grip that stops a hot magnet from quietly forgetting it was ever magnetised.
The physics ceiling is a real trade-off, not an engineering gap that funding closes: no rare-earth-free chemistry has both high magnetisation and high anisotropy at once. Iron nitride (Fe16N2, the material behind Niron Magnetics) has a saturation magnetisation that beats neodymium outright, but only about a fifth of its coercivity, and the phase decomposes above roughly 200-266C - https://www.nature.com/articles/srep25436, so the very heat that a rotor generates unmakes the magnet. Tetrataenite, the other hope, still cannot be made in bulk: the celebrated 2022 Cambridge "fast-ordering" recipe was retracted in December 2024 - https://retractionwatch.com/2025/10/08/cosmic-magnet-study-retracted-after-cleaning-agent-wipes-away-results/ after a cleaning agent, not the process, turned out to have produced the ordering. So the verdict weakens on the strong claim and holds on the refined one: the product works, just not as a neodymium-killer for cars. We went in asking whether a rare-earth-free magnet is bankable for EV traction in 2026-28; we now believe that at 33% confidence, down from 50%, because the coercivity and temperature limits are physics, not scale-up. Where iron nitride does win is as a good-enough replacement for cheap ferrite, in speakers, sensors, appliances, small motors, and increasingly defence, robotics and data-centre pumps. The tell is what Niron actually shipped: its first 2026 commercial product is a loudspeaker magnet with FaitalPRO - https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260120902354/en/, not a rotor. That is a genuine business almost nobody is pricing, and nothing like the EV story on the tin.
Now the gating layer, because this is where the "rare-earth crisis" money actually flows, and it splits in two. The China-free premium is real but bifurcated: the heavy rare earths dysprosium and terbium, the ones a traction motor cannot do without, carry an ex-China price 4-5x China's today, and Benchmark forecasts that gap widening to 8.3x by 2027 - https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/ex-china-rare-earths-premium-to-grow-especially-for-heavies. The bulk light rare earth NdPr spiked then fell about 35% once the trade truce eased supply fear - https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/012726-rare-earth-supply-bottlenecks-set-to-persist-in-2026. So the durable scarcity, and the durable rent, sits in the heavies and in who can process them outside China, not in the headline chemistry that claims to remove rare earths entirely.

The rivals that actually win the rare-earth problem are not new chemistry, they are recycling and motor redesign, and the buyers with real money already voted for them. On the recycling side, GM signed MP Materials for 1,000 t/yr of US magnet supply - https://mpmaterials.com/news/mp-materials-to-build-us-magnet-factory-enters-long-term-supply-agreement-with-general-motors/, Apple committed $500M, Nidec-Noveon is scaling, and Birmingham's HyProMag reports recycled N48 grade "near-identical to virgin" - https://mkango.ca/news/hypromag-advances-uk-magnet-manufacturing-and-customer-collaborations/, full performance with no rare-earth penalty. On redesign, BMW's 5th-generation current-excited motor runs at volume with no magnets at all - https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/usa/article/detail/T0327877EN_US/, and Renault-Valeo have a 200kW wound-rotor unit. Governments are underwriting the incumbent too, with the MP-DoD deal setting a 10-year $110/kg NdPr price floor plus a ~15% equity stake.
Bankability is the wall the strong thesis dies on: no rare-earth-free plant has raised a pound of project finance, it is all equity and grants. Niron has raised roughly $300M, with a Sartell 1,500t line targeted for ~2027 and a $1.8B, 10kt plant slated for 2028 - https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/niron-magnetics-15b-iron-nitride-magnets-plant-savills/814845/, yet there is no qualified traction design-in behind it, and Proterial's heavy-rare-earth-free NdFeB and Sm-Fe-N for pumps - https://www.proterial.com/e/press/2025/n0722b.html are the nearer, reduced-rare-earth signal. IDTechEx puts rare-earth-free at only ~12% of the magnet market by 2034. So what: the honest near-term market is reducing rare earths inside NdFeB and recycling them back, not removing them, and a first qualified traction design-in with a start-of-production date is the single event that would re-rate the strong thesis.
The AI angle is the sharpest test of whether machine discovery beats the synthesis wall, and so far it publishes cost and carbon but no magnetism. Materials Nexus (London) used an AI algorithm to design MagNex, a rare-earth-free magnet synthesised at the Henry Royce Institute in Sheffield - https://www.sheffield.ac.uk/royce-institute/case-studies/matnex-case-study, and reports 20% lower cost and 70% less carbon, but zero magnetic performance numbers, at seed stage. That silence is the tell: AI compresses the screening step, but making and qualifying the material is still the hard, slow part, and the screening layer itself is commoditising as tools like MatterGen go open-source. The durable value is not the screen; it is the synthesis IP and the Western manufacturing rights on the other side of it.

Ecosystem placement
Magnets are the physical bottleneck under the entire electrification, robotics and AI-data-centre build, so this is a chokepoint story, not a chemistry one. The rent goes to whoever controls processing, recycling and Western manufacturing, not the mine and not the headline chemistry. Every electric drivetrain, every humanoid actuator, every liquid-cooling pump for a GPU rack pulls on the same NdFeB supply chain, and that chain is gated at the heavy-rare-earth refining step that sits almost entirely in China. Rare-earth-free chemistry is one hedge against that chokepoint; recycling and magnet-free motor design are the other two, and today they are the ones with cash behind them. The magnet is where the AI and defence build meets the mineral map, which is why it prices on supply security now, not on climate.
The rare-earth-free magnet is being sold as the answer to the rare-earth crisis. It isn't. The physics caps it below neodymium exactly where cars need it, and the buyers with real money, GM, Apple and the Pentagon, voted for recycled neodymium and magnet-free motors instead. The bankable-now rare-earth-free business is replacing cheap ferrite in speakers and sensors, a genuine business nobody is pricing, and simultaneously nothing like the EV story on the tin.
