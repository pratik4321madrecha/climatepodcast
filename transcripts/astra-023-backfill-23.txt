The Spine · Workshop
Start with the wire itself, because the whole thesis rests on one material fact. A standard overhead transmission conductor (ACSR, aluminium-conductor-steel-reinforced) is aluminium strands wrapped around a steel core that carries the mechanical load. When you push more current through aluminium it heats, and hot aluminium expands and sags; sag into the legal ground-clearance limit is what caps how much power a given line can carry. A composite-core conductor swaps the steel for a carbon-fibre rod - https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2411207121 (ACCC, aluminium-conductor-composite-core, or AECC, aluminium-encapsulated-carbon-core). That carbon rod has a coefficient of thermal expansion, meaning how much it stretches per degree of heat, about 14x lower than aluminium (1.61 versus 23 millionths per degree C).
That single number is the product. Because the core barely grows when hot, the line can run at 180-200C instead of the ~75C ceiling of a steel core without drooping past clearance, so you can pack in about 28% more (annealed, softer) aluminium and push roughly 2x the current at 25-40% lower resistive loss. The analogy: same pipe, same right-of-way, but a liner that lets you run hotter fluid without the pipe sagging. This is not a lab result. It is running today on real towers - https://www.tdworld.com/overhead-transmission/article/21181394/ssen-transmission-extends-the-asset-life-and-capacity-of-existing-towers, including a 44km SSEN re-string at Fort Augustus in Scotland.

Settle two: the only venture-scale seat a non-technical founder can take is asset-light, because the wire and its installation are already owned. The composite core is a consolidating commodity: the foundational patent (EP1506085) was revoked, and Exel and TS Conductor lock up pultrusion and wrote the IEC 62818 spec - https://tratosgroup.com/news/tratos-signs-a-four-year-framework-agreement-with-exel-composites-to-support-grid-modernisation. In the UK even installation is incumbent-locked: National Grid handed its £1.2bn, 1,000km reconductoring programme to five framework EPCs - https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2026/06/23/five-firms-win-1-2bn-of-overhead-power-line-work/ (Balfour Beatty, M Group, Morgan Sindall, Murphy, and an Omexom-Taylor Woodrow JV) on 23 June 2026. Certification is a Kinectrics (Canada) near-monopoly with zero UK presence - https://www.tdworld.com/test-and-measurement/news/55360586/ts-conductor-to-pursue-first-kinectrics-certification-for-composite-core-htls-conductors.
"The wire is plumbing. The intelligence that decides how hard to run it is the open seat, and it isn't British yet."
That open seat is grid-enhancing-technologies software, specifically dynamic line rating: sensors plus algorithms that read weather and safely push more power on cool, windy days. National Grid's +585km dynamic-line-rating rollout runs on LineVision (US), Ampacimon (Belgium) and Heimdall (Norway) - https://www.nationalgrid.com/media-centre/press-releases/national-grid-installs-grid-enhancing-technology, worth up to £50M in consumer savings, with no UK champion. Utilities buy a portfolio, not one product: Georgia Power bundles dynamic line rating with reconductoring - https://www.linevisioninc.com/news/linevision-deploying-advanced-grid-technology-with-georgia-power, and the regulated owner captures the rent. This is the 8th instance of a repeating pattern (value migrates from the element to the integration), with a sharper corollary: where even the integration is EPC-captured, the only foothold is the software and services beside the build-out.
Settle three, the honest disconfirming layer: the win is bounded, so do not model it as clearing the queue. Reconductoring wins durable magnitude, not incremental congestion relief (that belongs to dynamic line rating at 5-7% of an upgrade's cost with sub-year payback). The addressable share is the ~50-65% of the interconnection shortfall that is thermal-limited; the rest re-binds on the transformer and substation gate, which is tightening (large-power-transformer lead times ran from 128 to 160+ weeks, prices up 77% since 2019 - https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/, new US factories only from 2027). And the ~2,061 GW LBNL queue shrank ~10% on withdrawals, not throughput - https://emp.lbl.gov/publications/queued-2026-edition-characteristics, so state the relief as capacity-share, never queue-clearing.

Where this sits in the whole system: reconductoring is the cheapest, fastest way to enlarge the existing wires while everything upstream and downstream stays slow. Generation (solar, storage, fusion) is racing ahead, and demand (AI data centres) is exploding, but the grid between them is the binding constraint. Reconductoring relaxes the thermal-limited part of that constraint on land utilities already control, buying years against the 5-10 year new-line timeline. It does not touch the transformer and substation bottleneck, which is why it is a partial, portfolio move inside a larger grid-capacity fight, not a silver bullet.
